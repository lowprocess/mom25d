# CM5 Minima U702 input-loop benchmark

The source is KiCad's CM5 Minima demo (CERN-OHL-S), downloaded separately from
the [KiCad demos](https://gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad/-/raw/master/demos/cm5_minima/CM5_MINIMA_3.kicad_pcb).
The verified source revision is
[`085c61811bbbbd21cc0801ca416541af8d15986c`](https://gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad/-/raw/085c61811bbbbd21cc0801ca416541af8d15986c/demos/cm5_minima/CM5_MINIMA_3.kicad_pcb).
The board is not redistributed here. Its SHA-256 and the numerical settings
are recorded in the [fine](cm5-u702-fine.json) and [coarse](cm5-u702-coarse.json)
reports. The fine report used an exported board JSON; both refer to the same
source KiCad file. Coordinates are kicad2ems model coordinates (Y inverted).

```sh
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=2 python tools/benchmark_converter.py CM5_MINIMA_3.kicad_pcb \
  --fine 0.6 --out cm5-fine.json
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=2 python tools/benchmark_converter.py CM5_MINIMA_3.kicad_pcb \
  --fine 0.8 --out cm5-coarse.json
```

The explicit input loop is U702's +5V to its GND pads, with C704, C705 and
C706 connected through their copper. All six copper layers are included for
the selected +5V/GND nets, cropped to the parts' extent plus 3 mm. Capacitor
values come from the board; ESR 3 mOhm, ESL 0.4 nH and package field heights
are benchmark assumptions. The source is 1 A. Magnetic DC-sheet loss is used;
these results do not validate the new slab or dielectric models on this board.

| Quantity | Fine 0.6 mm | Coarse 0.8 mm |
|---|---:|---:|
| Triangles | 9,736 | 8,889 |
| RWG edges | 11,709 | 10,462 |
| Via segments | 85 | 85 |
| Loops | 2,914 | 2,515 |
| Assembly + three-frequency solve | 66.8 s | 69.0 s |
| Converged FFT scan | 0.35 s | 1.25 s |
| FFT internal refinement | 2 | 4 |
| Sampled direct-field peak-relative difference | 0.92% | 0.22% |
| Retained copper matrices | 1.10 GB | 0.88 GB |
| Copper R at 1 kHz | 5.60 mOhm | 5.75 mOhm |
| Copper L at 1 kHz | 2.230 nH | 2.242 nH |
| Copper L at 1 MHz | 1.193 nH | 1.218 nH |
| Copper L at 10 MHz | 1.373 nH | 1.425 nH |

Wall timings were recorded under different host loads; the small timing
difference between meshes is not a performance conclusion. The field scan is
17 by 17 points at two heights (1 and 3 mm above top copper), three
frequencies, requiring successive-grid agreement within 2% at every height
and frequency. Direct checks use a 5 by 5 subset at both heights.

The previous storage layout would retain approximately 4.4 GB for the fine
case's dense copper/branch R and L copies, versus the measured 1.10 GB of
copper blocks now. This is an allocation-size comparison, not a measured
peak-RSS comparison: triangle moments and solve workspaces still increase
peak memory.

The mesh comparison changes loop L by up to 3.8% and DC R by 2.7%. These
results establish a reproducible real-board workload; they do not establish
mesh convergence, crop convergence, agreement with hardware, or an accurate
package model. Increase the crop and refine further before relying on
absolute values. Whole-board compression remains future work; this cropped
case already runs without it.

## Parallel fill update

The same input JSON and 0.6 mm fine mesh were rerun with the OpenMP backend.
The raw reports retain the pinned board revision and SHA-256:
[one thread](cm5-u702-openmp-one.json), [two threads](cm5-u702-openmp-two.json).

| Quantity | Previous serial fill | OpenMP, 1 thread | OpenMP, 2 threads |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Moment fill | not recorded separately | 12.65 s | 9.56 s |
| Assembly + three-frequency sweep | 66.8 s | 25.5 s | 22.4 s |
| Retained copper matrices | 1.10 GB | 1.10 GB | 1.10 GB |

The native runtime probe observed the requested one/two threads. BLAS used
two threads for both sweeps. R/L agrees with the previous result within
1.4e-14 relative; one- and two-thread OpenMP results are identical. The
numerical agreement checks implementation consistency, not physical accuracy.
These single runs have host-load uncertainty; the synthetic backend benchmark
records repeated samples. New controls and extension contracts are in the
[backend guide](../static-backends.md).
